Spray only. Cone droplets. Close = dense; far = overspray.

Stick
Contact

Dry media: friction on paper tooth. Soft sticks drop more dust. Side of the stick makes a wide ellipse. No wet drips.

Motion
Paper

Abrasive: cuts pigment back to the ground. Fine grit = many short scratches. Coarse = fewer, deeper. Linear follows the stroke; circular swirls.

Graphite

H = hard, light, thin. B = soft, dark, wide. Point = line; on side = shade. Shine = graphite burnish (lighter streak when you press).

Ink
Nib

Alcohol dye in felt. Overlaps stack darker. Chisel: thick/thin by angle. Bleed is paper capillary, not a drip. Reservoir dries along the stroke.

Liquid

Drag A → B on the sphere, release to impact. A is the hit; B is throw direction and speed. Short drag = drop from above. Thin body (coke) runs farther; thick (blood/paint) stays in lobes.

Particle

Stroke lays a glue film. Dust, glitter and rust rain onto that film by themselves — no extra click. Glitter = PET flakes lying on the glue, one face mirrors the light. Rust grows as oxide scabs. Rate is how fast the film loads.

Category

Soot = carbon stain. Smoke = live fluid on the sphere. It only stains where the cloud has slowed (not at the source). Flakes = how much of that slow smoke sticks. Fast wisps stay airborne.

Stain

Film stain on the coat — oil and paint marks. Tilt leans gravity. This is not pouring water.

Fluid

Hold to emit liquid on the sphere. Beads stick to the surface and run downhill (gravity + viscosity). Water is runny; oil crawls and pools at the bottom. This is 3D fluid on the mesh, not a stain stamp.
