What ReSTIR does

Direct lighting is dominated by a handful of bright configurations, so 1-sample Monte Carlo wastes most rays. ReSTIR (Bitterli et al., SIGGRAPH 2020) applies Resampled Importance Sampling per pixel and keeps the result in a reservoir — a constant-size summary (chosen sample y, candidates seen M, weight W) that can be merged with any other reservoir in O(1). That single property is what makes temporal + spatial reuse of one light sample affordable.

Algorithm here

1 · initial candidates by analytic light-source sampling (quads/discs, power-weighted) — target PDF p̂ ∝ luminance of the unshadowed contribution (reconnection-style). 2 · temporal merge via motion reprojection of static geometry. 3 · k spatial rounds, 3 jittered taps each, shift ratio p̂x(ys)/p̂s(ys) clamped to [0.05, 20]. 4 · final visibility ray + shade. Merging rescales ΣW proportionally when M hits the cap — the standard staleness/leak control (see also Kettunen et al. 2021, “Rearchitecting Spatiotemporal Resampling for Production”).

Bias & cost knobs

Visibility is decoupled from resampling by default (~2 rays/px — fast, mildly biased at shadow boundaries). STRICT SHIFTS re-tests occlusion on every merge (+1 ray/merge, removes most leaks). M-CAP bounds how much history one pixel may trust.

Data structures

WebGPU has no hardware ray-query API (no TLAS/BLAS), so this demo builds a binned-SAH BVH (8 bins, leaf = 3) on the CPU at load/rebuild and traverses it entirely on the GPU: iterative stackless DFS, near-child-first push, Möller–Trumbore leaf tests. Analytic spheres are intersected in closed form alongside the BVH. Reservoirs are SoA 2×vec4 (32 B/px) ping-pong buffers; every stage is 8×8 compute.

Further reading / next steps

Ouyang et al. 2021 “ReSTIR GI” (path resampling), full shift mappings (replay/hybrid), NEE hybrids, and hardware RT once WebGPU exposes ray queries.
